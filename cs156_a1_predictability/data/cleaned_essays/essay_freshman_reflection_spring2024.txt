I have always been scared of being average. I love history. Anyone afforded space in a history book is anything but average. In a world proliferated with information, prime-time real estate is occupied by unique individuals, from technological trendsetters to activists championing what they believe in. I have always wanted to stand out in my own way, driven by my interests.. The working definition of my purpose statement would be to cultivate values and skills essential for embracing individuality while being uncomfortable and ordinary, drawing inspiration from successful figures. Its vagueness in what exactly I should be doing is by design. My statement allows me to explore interests as long as I seek improvement. It is also broadly applicable as I am immersed in different situations as I juggle school, social life, and career development. From the get-go, I want to recognize that my purpose statement isn’t the most compatible with leadership. In high school, I tended to dismiss student leadership like all hard-headed teenagers. I wanted to make it different here. In my CCP, our group never established formal leadership as we wanted to value everyone’s opinion. However, we shared the same purpose in creating a deliverable to our partner that they can integrate into their mission on conflict de-escalation. Though the best way to judge leadership is through the end goal, we have had several milestones that can paint a picture of our effectiveness. From the onset, Brady stood out as he had experience with organizations dealing with conflict. Even more impressive was that he knew a friend of our CCP partner. This afforded him power through expertise. Tigran, another team member, had a methodological way of thinking and had experience working with a tech non-profit. He had a penchant for specifics and is one of the most natural #breakitdown-ers. This afforded him bureaucratic power as he always kept us on track to get to the final deliverable. Brady provided the destination, and Tigran ensured we were as close to ETA as possible. Our group itself was very enthusiastic, and everyone was motivated. This proved effective the first few months as we were just ideating. However, it proved difficult when we had to develop the idea as the planning fallacy became more apparent. However, it also became clear that Tigran expected the same deadlines to be met regardless of assignment schedules. I, therefore, took the initiative to apply expressive leadership in the meetings. I did this as I noticed my role as the meeting planner gave me the chance to dictate the tone of the meeting. I would, therefore, always start by giving everyone a chance to say how their week was without the CCP project. This illuminated the state everyone was in and made Tigran notice why some milestones were not met in some weeks. This also made our bond stronger as a group, as we knew each other more holistically. We then adjusted our end deliverable to be less demanding but still valuable to our partner as we saw the weeks could only get busier. When I set up the agenda to include the weekly check-up, we noticed Tigran had been very late on his assignments and was missing quite several classes. However, he often dismissed them. He said he had them under control and that the academic committee had given him some allowance. Though concerning, I found his words almost reassuring. Though I wanted to talk to him more about his situation, I was worried that he might see me as an intrusive colleague, and I didn’t want to destroy the good rapport and friendship I thought I was building with him. I, therefore, always pushed this conversation to a later date due to my introversion. I believe I also acted cowardly as I didn’t involve other people or fill out the anonymous forms Minerva avails, as I thought this would be another break of trust on him. I also didn’t know how I could support him. My only suggestions were that he reduce his CCP workload and focus on his classes. However, I tried to build some level of trust between us so that when push comes to shove, he would see my actions as well intended and that our CCP wouldn’t suffer. This proved to be my failure as I continually saw that the number of extensions and absences increased, yet I didn’t act. There are also risks of getting hurt by the crowd and the ridiculous cost of treatment here in the USA. However, I simply couldn’t be indifferent to the events anymore. I was intimidated by the heavy police presence on Dec. 12th, 2023. Of all things, a student is responsible for their grades. As an academic, my outcome index matters most. I personally set a goal to be among the pacesetters before coming here. However, my fall semester wasn’t as easy. I felt I was always behind. I felt my responses were always unpolished. I didn’t like my grades (they were not bad, but there was room for improvement). However, I saw this was mostly within my internal locus of control. I saw this issue could be solved on two fronts. Firstly, I approached Cigna (Minerva’s official insurance partner) and organized a counseling session with an academic professional. This was the first time I scheduled a counseling session, and it proved helpful. The counselor was able to give me tips like boxed breathing and more exercise to reduce anxiety and suggested I focus more on my performance as she saw I was too self-critical. Secondly, I took the time this semester to visit the first office hours with the professors whose courses I struggled with a bit last semester. I asked about the expectations and common student pitfalls in the past. I expressed my concern that my effort didn’t seem to translate to my grades and shared my previous assignments. I received precious feedback on how to handle assignments and trying to be objective. In EA, for example, I usually felt I submitted a quality assignment only to be underwhelmed by the results. With the guidance of my professors and the Cigna counselor, I have been able to produce quality assignments this semester. The highlight was an email from my EA professor highlighting a “wonderful assignment.” This also manifested when more and more classmates started asking me questions after class due to my contributions, which indicated an improvement in my responses in class.
